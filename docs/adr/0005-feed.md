# ADR 0005: Feed and reels

The feed policy in `mpx_player` decides what plays, with sound or not, and what
preloads in a scrolling surface; the list and pager widgets that apply it live
in `mpx_ui`. It has two modes on one knob set: `list`, where every
visible clip plays muted, and `pager`, where one item plays with sound and its
neighbours stay warm. Preload is staged by distance and direction. The policy
reads no platform state; signals come through one host seam, and `mpx`'s
device signals are their default source.

**Status**: Accepted (decided with the maintainer 2026-11-10)
**Relates to**: 0001, 0003, 0004, 0010
**Amended**: 2026-11-13, package homes (ticket 27); 2026-11-14, headless
feed seam (ticket 49); 2026-11-17, loading stages (ADR 0010); 2026-11-18,
the pager widget on the viewer's gallery pager (ADR 0006)

## Context

In the messenger clients' chat and channel history no item is "active": every
visible video autoplays muted on the software decoder and loops, and frames
advance only when painted (Android stops advancing below 25% visible). One
player app-wide has sound; a round video with sound moves to PiP when it
scrolls off. Stories, the reels analogue, play one item, keep ±1 warm and up
to two prepared players, gated to high-class devices with at least 8 HEVC
decoder instances. Preload is windowed and staged by distance; neither mobile
client predicts from scroll velocity. Media3 and Meta practice agree on one
active item, ±1 neighbours and back-off under stress.

## Decision

### Modes

| Mode | Behaviour | Player tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `list` | Every visible clip plays muted and loops; frames advance only when painted | Software decoders (`mpx_video_decoder`); a player only for the sound owner |
| `pager` | One active item with sound; ±1 warm; at most 2 prepared players, taken over on swipe | `mpx_player` pool |

One knob set serves both, with per-mode defaults.

### Knobs

The 25 knobs of research `05-feed-policies` §7 are the seam, each
with its default; the host overrides any of them. Groups:

| Group | Knobs (research numbering) |
| --- | --- |
| Selection | 1 mode, 2 active-item rule |
| Autoplay and sound | 3 autoplay gate (`none` / `prefixOnly` / `full`), 4 size limit, 5 muted autoplay, 6 sound owner, 7 initial mute, 8 off-screen behaviour of the sound owner, 9 auto-advance |
| Preload | 10 window, 11 stage by distance, 12 oversize prefix (a duration), 13 cancel on leave, 24 story-tray preload |
| Players | 14 prepared pool size, 15 start buffer, 16 forward buffer cap |
| Memory | 17 heavy-part unload distance, 18 inline resolution cap |
| Quality and stress | 19 quality choice, 20 stress back-off, 21 pause layers |
| Per surface | 22 resume position, 23 loop |
| Diagnostics | 25 hooks |

The sound owner (6) and the prepared-player budget (14) come from
the player pool (ADR 0004), in the same package; the feed fills the policy.

Amended 2026-11-15 (review fixes): `FeedPolicy` carries only the knobs
code reads. Knobs 15, 16, 18 and 19 return with a native player backend
(tickets 23, 24, 28), 8 with picture in picture and background audio
(ticket 29), 24 and 25 with their first consumer; 6 is not a knob: the pool
is the one sound owner (ADR 0004). Of the kept ones, 7 seeds the pager's
mute (following the ringer through `FeedConditions.silenced` until the user
toggles), 17 releases a cell more than that many viewports from view
(`FeedVisibility.viewportsAway`, `FeedItemState.released`), and 20 and 21
are read from the scope's clock: no preload starts, or nothing plays.

### Preload

| Area | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Staging | By distance and direction: next item 5 s, previous 3 s, further items metadata only |
| Cancel | Everything outside the window is cancelled |
| Velocity | Only stretches the window ahead for the cheap stages (metadata, first frame). Nothing heavy starts during a fast scroll |
| Oversize | A duration prefix, resolved to bytes through the container index (ADR 0002 fast start) |

### Signals

| Area | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Seam | `FeedSignals` in `mpx_player` takes the host's `ValueListenable`s (network class, power saving, thermal state; mapped by name from `mpx`'s enums) and yields one `FeedConditions` snapshot; file size is on each `FeedItem`. It also yields `metered`, `powerSaving` and `stressed` for the core's `PlaybackActivity` |
| Default adapter | Device signals in `mpx` (Android, iOS, macOS) expose network class, power-saving mode and thermal status as `ValueListenable`s |
| Wiring | No package below `mpx` depends on them. The stack feeds the core's pause layers (`lowPower`); the host passes them to this seam and anything else |
| Rules | The autoplay rule and the size limits are host knobs with defaults |

### Headless seam

Playback works inside any host scrollable, not only the feed widgets.
Mechanism in `mpx_player`, convenience in `mpx_ui`:

| Piece | Package | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FeedController` | `mpx_player` | Runs the planner of its mode (one strategy per mode, no mode branches in the controller), holds the preloads (through the core's scope) and the pager's leases (through the pool), and exposes each item's `FeedItemState` (fraction, in view, autoplay, playing, muted, preload stage, lease) as a `ValueListenable`; `inView` is the list's item in view or the pager's playing page. Registrations carry a measure callback; the controller measures them all in one post-frame pass per frame that asked |
| `FeedSlot`, `FeedScope` | `mpx_player` | The slot registers its index and `FeedItem` and measures its own box with the core's `ViewportVisibility` (shared with the viewer origin): clipped by the closest `RenderAbstractViewport`, then by every viewport around it (nested lists). No extent, axis or viewport type assumed; a box a parent does not paint (kept alive off screen) is hidden. It asks for a measure after its own layout and when any enclosing `ScrollPosition` moves |
| `FeedClip`, `FeedListView`, `FeedPagerView` | `mpx_ui` | `FeedClip` draws a slot's state (a `PlayerView` while it holds a lease, else the cached clip at its fraction); the two views are a `ListView` / `GalleryPager` of slots and duplicate nothing of the controller |

In pager mode the page that plays is the most visible above the active-item
threshold (`FeedActiveItemRule.pick`, the same choice as the list's item in
view); below the threshold the previous page keeps playing. The scroll
direction is the move of the visible items' fraction-weighted index
centroid, so it needs no axis or offset. Items the host has no facts for
(no registration, no `itemOf`) are neither played, warmed nor preloaded.

### Posters

A clip's poster is the core's staged view (preview, thumbnail, first
frame); the player's stand-in is that view. Pager mode shows the buffering
spinner after the viewer policy's delay (ADR 0010).

### Core additions this needs

A visible fraction on bindings, staged preload requests with priority and
cancel, network and power inputs on the pause layers (ADR 0003).

## Why

- The split (many cheap muted loops, one expensive player) is what
  keeps a long history smooth; a single active item in a chat list would stop
  every other clip.
- Staging by distance is where the messenger clients and Media3 agree; the maintainer
  allowed velocity to stretch only cheap stages because they cost bytes, not
  decoders.
- A host seam for signals keeps the package testable and lets a host that
  already tracks network or power feed it without a second plugin.
- Signals in the composition root serve the feed, the core's pause layers and
  any other consumer without making any of them depend on a platform plugin.
- The policy is rules over the player pool (prepared budget, sound owner,
  preload stages), so it lives with the pool; widgets are optional UI beside
  the viewer.

## Rejected

| Alternative | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| One active item in lists | Stops all other visible clips |
| Velocity-driven heavy preload | Starts decoders the user scrolls past |
| Fixed byte prefixes | The clients' own lesson: express a prefix as duration |
| The feed reading platform state directly | Untestable; duplicates host state |
| Warmed player per visible item | Decoder-instance limits; Meta moved away from it |
| The adapter inside `mpx_player` | Ties device reads to one consumer; the core's pause layers need them too |
| Policy and widgets in one feed package | A package that is half pool rules, half UI |

## Consequences

- A host with lists only still takes `mpx_player` for the sound owner and the
  policy, and `mpx_ui` for the widgets unless it draws its own.
- Hosts without `mpx` bring their own signals.

## Open in implementation

- Defaults the clients do not give: the active-item visible fraction (0.5
  is a suggestion, not a given value).
- Device-class detection on Apple (the Android gate is high device class and
  ≥8 HEVC instances).
- Mute behaviour from the ringer / silent switch on iOS and the volume-key
  unmute: knob 7 holds the rule; whether the platform read joins `mpx`'s
  device signals is not decided.
- The list widget is `FeedListView` (items of any extent, measured by their
  slots; see *Headless seam*); the policy is `FeedPolicy.list` / `.pager`. The
  pager is `FeedPager` (mpx_player: leases the active item and up to
  min(knob 14, the pool's prepared budget) neighbours inside the window,
  nearest first, ahead before behind; a swipe plays the warm lease; an
  active item the autoplay gate holds back, `none`, gets no lease, since a
  prepared player would fetch the bytes of an item waiting for a tap, and
  one that held a lease when the gate closed keeps it paused) and
  `FeedPagerView` (mpx_ui: a `GalleryPager` of slots whose `FeedClip` shows
  the `PlayerView`, with the poster as stand-in, and one centre control:
  the buffering spinner holds back while the item's status control shows). The device-class gate on the prepared budget is the host's
  `PlayerPool.budget` (ticket 28).
- A viewport that resizes along its axis without scrolling or relaying out
  its items moves them without telling the slots; the host calls
  `FeedController.markNeedsMeasure` then.
- The cap and measure of the velocity stretch: v1 has no stretch; a fast
  scroll (the core's `fastScroll` layer) only downgrades heavy stages to
  metadata.

## Amendment 2026-11-18: the pager on the gallery pager

`FeedPagerView` pages on `GalleryPager` (ADR 0006), the viewer's own pager,
along its `scrollDirection`, with `GalleryPagerPhysics.feed` (a drag pages past
10 px when more along the axis than across; half a page or 400 px/s goes on;
a 300 ms settle; a third of the drag past the ends) and no dismiss and no
zoom. The pages move by transforms, so the pager's window is a
`RenderAbstractViewport` the slots measure against, and the view asks the
controller to measure again as the pager's position moves, as a scrollable
does; the most visible page above the threshold plays, mid-drag included, as
before. `onAdvance` slides with the pager's settle; an advance asked while a
slide runs is dropped (the page that took over mid-slide reporting its own
state). The view keeps no page built that does not show
(`GalleryKeepAlive.none`): the controller holds the neighbours' players.

## Amendment 2026-11-23: lite mode, and what the power saver is

The first gate stopped every autoplay while the device "saved power",
which a host fed with the system's battery saver, and the feed's pausing
layers paused what the reader played too. The usual clients do neither:

- Autoplay is the reader's setting per kind (videos, GIFs). That comes first;
  an item on the device does not override it.
- The power saver is a battery percentage (10 by default, configurable; 0
  never, 100 always), not the system's mode. At or under it nothing autoplays;
  a tap still plays. The system's battery saver counts only when the host says
  so.
- Low power is not a pausing layer of the feed. It still holds back preloads.

`FeedPolicy.lite` (`FeedLiteMode`), `FeedConditions.batteryLevel` and
`IDeviceSignals.batteryLevel` carry it.

## Amendment 2026-11-25: the sound of a clip in a list is the volume of its player

The sound switch of a list gave a clip a player and drew the player in place
of the clip's loop, and switching it off gave the loop back: two decoders that
drifted apart, a badge that counted the loop's time, and a player nobody
resumed after the app or a tab went away. The host's inline sound now:

- keeps the player on the clip when the sound is switched off (volume 0): one
  track, so the picture and the time do not change hands;
- starts the player where the clip's loop is (`seekPlayback`'s counterpart:
  `playbackPosition`);
- pauses the player under the feed's pausing layers and plays it again when
  they end, and gives it back when the clip leaves the view or its tab is
  hidden (`TickerMode`);
- unmutes on a rise of the system media volume (`IDeviceSignals.mediaVolume`):
  clients do it on a volume key (they intercept the key; a
  Flutter host sees the volume change, which a key and the slider both make).


## Amendment 2026-11-26: a clip the gate holds offers to play

A clip that autoplay leaves at its first frame (a setting, the power saver, a
size over the limit) showed nothing to say it plays, and a tap on it was only
the host's. The usual clients draw a play button over such a clip, and
something else for each of the other states of the same tile. The tile's
control is a function of two facts the layers already hold, so it is one
mechanism and not a branch per kind:

- the **transfer** (core): download while it waits for the reader, cancel and
  the arc while it runs, nothing once local;
- whether the clip **plays by itself** (the feed): `FeedItemState.autoplay` is
  `none` or `prefixOnly` for a held clip (a clip paused for a moment by a
  layer has `full` and is not held).

| State | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Local, plays | none |
| Local, held | play in the centre (`FeedClip.onPlay`) |
| Not local, waits for the reader | download, with the size |
| Not local, downloading | cancel and the arc (unknown total: a short arc turning, then growing) |
| Streamable video, waits for the reader | play in the centre (streams) and a compact download in the corner |

The badge is the duration, or "GIF", over any of them; a round video's control
is the round size. What a tap on play does is the host's: a GIF plays where it
is (`FeedController.requestPlay(index)`, for as long as it shows), a video
opens in the viewer. A host with no action gives none and gets no button. A
pager page is its own player: its host shows it (`FeedPager.show`).

The arc that turns without a progress is the platform's spinner: the head
grows accelerating, the start jumps by the longest sweep and the tail
decelerates onto the head (`MediaSpinnerMotion.chase`); a style keeps the sine
(`swing`) where its platform's spinner is not that.
